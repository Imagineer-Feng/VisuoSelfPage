Embodied Self-Recognition

Can an AI tell which body it controls?

Recognizing the world is one thing. Recognizing your own control is another. We investigate whether multimodal language models can distinguish self-generated motion from external action.

The central idea

From relative selection
to independent self-attribution.

An embodied agent should recognize the sensory consequences of its own actions, even without another body to compare against.

VisuoSelf pairs complete motor-command traces with ordered visual evidence. Models either choose the controlled arm from two candidates or judge a single arm as self or non-self. The underlying behaviors are matched, allowing us to test whether a successful comparison transfers to an independent judgment.

Choice · two arms

“Which arm is me?”

Requires a relative ranking.
→
Judgment · one arm

“Is this arm me?”

Requires a self/non-self boundary.

We study functional agency attribution: identifying what is under an agent’s control. The mirror in Figure 1 is conceptual, rather than an experimental setup.

Controlled evidence. Matched decisions.

A benchmark built around causality.

Three interventions separate command–motion correspondence, mapping stability, and temporal delay. Difficulty changes the non-self behavior while the self process stays fixed.

Does each motion follow the current command?

Same-step causality

The self arm executes the current command. The non-self arm reorders the same eight-command multiset, preserving total motion while breaking stepwise correspondence.

The key finding

Choosing correctly does not guarantee
knowing what is self.

Strong relative selection can coexist with near-chance isolated judgment and a tendency to accept almost any arm as self.

Cross-format transfer

Correct selection can fail to transfer to both matched isolated judgments. Aggregate accuracy alone misses this inconsistency.

Physical validation

Complementary real-robot recordings include 108 judgment episodes and 1,152 steps. Self-attribution bias also persists in physical recordings.
